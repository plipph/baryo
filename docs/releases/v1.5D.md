# Version 1.5D — Engagement

## Overview

Version 1.5D adds opt-in, profile-centered engagement around existing businesses and offers. It is planned, not implemented. The release must build on the profile foundation and any adopted offer/claim model.

## Goals

- Notify profiles about relevant, permitted platform events.
- Let profiles follow businesses and save offers.
- Provide a restrained activity feed that improves local discovery without becoming a general social network.

## Scope

- In-app notifications with read state and preference controls.
- Profile-to-business following.
- Saved offers for eligible authenticated profiles.
- Activity-feed events with defined sources, ordering, privacy, and retention.
- Delivery, rate-limit, abuse, and RLS policies appropriate to each engagement type.

## Database Changes

Planned data includes `notifications`, follow relationships, saved-offer relationships, and event records only if they are required for the activity feed. Define recipient identity, source event, visibility, deduplication, read state, retention, and indexes through migrations. Reuse offer/profile references rather than creating parallel identity models.

## UI Changes

- Account-level notifications and preferences.
- Follow controls on approved business surfaces.
- Saved-offer controls and list states.
- A focused feed of local-business activity, with empty/error/loading states and clear source labels.

## Routes Affected

Profile/account routes established in Version 1.5A gain engagement views. `/discover` and `/{slug}` may gain follow/save affordances. New notification or feed routes require approved design; no existing route is renamed.

## Components Affected

Profile/account components, public business/storefront components, offer components, notification/feed presentation, and server-side event/permission helpers.

## Acceptance Criteria

- A profile receives only notifications permitted by its preferences and authorization.
- Following and saved offers are private unless a later product decision explicitly exposes them.
- Feed entries come only from documented event sources and respect visibility/expiry.
- Users can mark notifications read and manage relevant preferences.
- Engagement actions cannot expose protected business, profile, or claim data.
- Migration, RLS, lint, build, privacy, and event-deduplication tests pass.

## Out of Scope

Direct messages, user-generated posts, open commenting, real-time chat, a general social graph, email/push delivery unless separately approved, and unrelated admin tooling.

## Risks

Notifications and feeds can become noisy, leak sensitive activity, or create load through fan-out. Following and saved offers require privacy-safe defaults. Feed design can drift toward an unmoderated social network without strict source constraints.

## Migration Strategy

Build only after profile identity is stable and after the relevant business/offer events are defined. Start with in-app records, feature-flag the experience, seed no private activity, monitor volume and delivery correctness, then consider additional channels only through a new specification.

## Future Compatibility

The engagement model should support municipality-aware community updates and future verified organization/government events, while preserving profile consent and avoiding a separate social-network architecture.
